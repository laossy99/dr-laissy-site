01 — About

Engineer, educator, and academic leader — building structures and the programs that train those who design them.

Dr. Mohamed Laissy is an Associate Professor of Civil Engineering at the University of Prince Mugrin (UPM) in Medina, Saudi Arabia, and the former Head of the Civil Engineering Department (2017–2024). He currently chairs the College of Engineering Curriculum Committee and the Department Quality Assurance Committee. His career spans more than thirty years across structural engineering practice and academia — including fifteen years of service with the Egyptian Army Forces, rising to Chief Officer Engineer, followed by academic appointments in Egypt, Libya, and Saudi Arabia.

His research centres on the seismic behaviour of reinforced concrete buildings — particularly on sloped terrain in Saudi Arabia — the design of projectile-resistant concrete using ceramics, fibres, and high-toughness materials, and the strengthening and preservation of existing structures such as the historic Makhet Castle on the Hejaz Railway.

As former department head (2017–2024) and a member of UPM's University Council, he leads curriculum design, program-level assessment, and academic accreditation — chairing the College of Engineering Curriculum Committee and the Department Quality Assurance Committee, serving as an internal auditor and NCAAA accreditation reviewer, and contributing to the institutional accreditation UPM was awarded in 2022.

02 — Research

Where structures, seismic risk, and materials meet.

A cohesive program studying how reinforced concrete buildings behave under seismic and impact loads, how existing structures can be preserved and strengthened, and how construction and education can be modernized through digital and AI tools.

01

Seismic Response of RC Buildings on Sloped Terrain

Comparative studies of concrete slab systems, shear walls with openings, bracing systems, and thermal effects on multistory RC buildings on sloped terrain — published in Civil Engineering Journal and Engineering, Technology & Applied Science Research.

  • Seismic
  • RC buildings
  • Sloped terrain
02

Projectile-Resistant Concrete

Experimental and numerical investigation of concrete blocks reinforced with ceramics, fibres, Teflon sheets, and high-toughness energy-absorbing layers to resist high-velocity projectile penetration.

  • Impact loading
  • Composite materials
  • Numerical modelling
03

Structural Strengthening & Restoration

Strengthening of existing RC school buildings in Medina and structural & cultural restoration of the historic Makhet Castle on the Hejaz Railway — bridging engineering practice with heritage preservation.

  • Strengthening
  • Heritage
  • RC buildings
04

Sustainable & 3D-Printed Construction

Emerging construction technologies — 3D printing for building infrastructure and sustainable restoration practices — presented at ICSDI 2024 (Riyadh) and applied in Saudi road-project time-overrun studies.

05 — Teaching

Structural engineering education, from first principles to accredited practice.

Teaching bridges Dr. Laissy's research and departmental leadership. Courses emphasize first-principles mechanics, code-based design (SBC 302, ACI, Eurocode), and the engineering judgement students need on real projects — reinforced by digital tools, formative assessment, and ABET/NCAAA-aligned learning outcomes.

Teaching Philosophy

  • First principles, then code. Students derive behaviour from mechanics before applying SBC 302, ACI 318, or Eurocode.
  • Design as engineering judgement. Coursework mirrors real projects — sloped sites, seismic zones, existing buildings.
  • Formative over summative. Rubrics, digital assessment, and continuous feedback (per UPM 2024 training).
  • AI as a tool, not a shortcut. ChatGPT-augmented instruction that strengthens reasoning, not replaces it.
  • Outcomes aligned to accreditation. ABET/NCAAA-mapped learning outcomes and CQI cycles.
